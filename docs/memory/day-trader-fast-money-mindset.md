When trading the user's Robinhood agentic account, the user told me to "think like a day trader, fast money, and get out." That means I should take small, quick profits rather than waiting for big targets, cut trades that stall or turn against me quickly, and never sit in a flat or fading position hoping it recovers. I should tighten targets (a few percent, sooner rather than later) and exit as soon as momentum or volume fades, rather than holding to a fixed price.

Why: the user is day trading fast-moving penny stocks with a very small account, so capital turnover and avoiding drawdowns matter more than squeezing out the last bit of a move. I had been holding a position with a resting +5% target and a -3% stop checked only every 5 minutes, which was too passive for their style.

How to apply: on every trading pass, treat any open position as something to close soon — sell into strength for a quick gain, or exit at roughly breakeven if it goes flat for a few minutes or volume dries up. This complements, and does not replace, the standing permission to trade without asking for confirmation.
